About

Where I'm headed

I'm a Sociology major at Tulane University with minors in Political Science and Economics. Law draws me for the same reasons student government and campaign work do: rules and structures shape people's lives, and someone has to read them closely, argue about them honestly, and improve them.

At Tulane I've gone from delegate to Co-Chair of the Undergraduate Assembly, where I lead the body and act as a liaison to faculty and administration. I also serve on the University Senate. Off campus I've made thousands of calls for a record-setting state senate campaign and spent three summers responsible for kids' safety in the backcountry. Across all of it, I've found that the job comes down to preparing carefully, communicating clearly, and following through for other people.

Featured highlights

Four stories that show how I work

Ethan for Kansas · Summer 2026

Helping raise a record $2.2 million

The situation: Senator Ethan Corson's campaign needed to build support at every level, from national donors to neighbors on the doorstep.

What I did: As a fundraising intern I made thousands of cold calls, researched potential donors at the local, state, and national level, and helped prepare the candidate's letters to supporters and undecided voters. I also canvassed nearly 200 homes, almost all of them in our primary opponents' districts, and delivered more than 100 yard signs.

The result: Our team raised $2.2 million, setting Kansas fundraising records and outraising our primary opponents combined.

Tulane Undergraduate Assembly · 2026–Present

Reforming how student government governs itself

The situation: An assembly that represents the whole student body only works if its own structure is sound.

What I did: After my fellow delegates elected me Co-Chair, I headed the Governance & Internal Affairs working group and co-led the Summer Transitions Committee. The working group reviewed TUA's structure and wrote its findings up as formal recommendations.

The result: Several reports recommending significant reforms to TUA's structure. I now lead biweekly Assemblies and Council of Chairs meetings and meet regularly with the Office of Student Affairs.

Tulane Undergraduate Assembly · Spring 2026

Starting Project Pulse

The situation: Delegates were doing real work, but most students never heard about it.

What I did: As a Communications Committee delegate I started Project Pulse, a social media series in which delegates talk about the projects they're working on. I also made and shared material promoting TUA events.

The result: Students got a regular, direct look at what their representatives were doing, which built awareness of TUA and made the organization easier to reach.

Camp Sabra · 2023–2025

Leading when the stakes are real

The situation: In the Masada unit, each session ends with a two-week backpacking trip across Colorado. As one of the few staff members on site, I was responsible for campers' safety miles from help.

What I did: I co-led 70 campers over two sessions, specializing in programming and community care. I handled camper emergencies and coordinated with park officials, bus drivers, off-site supervisors, and law enforcement. I worked my way up from counselor to Assistant Unit Head (overseeing five cabins) to Masada staff.

The result: Safe trips and campers who were supported throughout. I received the Terri Grossman Neshama Award as the camp's male staff member of the year, and I was selected as an FJC Cornerstone Fellow.
